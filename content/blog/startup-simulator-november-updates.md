If you played [Startup Simulator](/work/startup-sim/) when it first landed, fair warning: the last few days of updates weren’t polish-only. The game got heavier in places I’ve wanted to go since launch, and a couple of fixes were just “please stop showing me $20754.58B.”

**Your CEO is a person now.** There’s a CEO tab. Company cash and your personal bank are separate. Payroll can hit your salary automatically; you can set a living budget and watch owner draws, equity, and public-company liquidity (when you get there) show up in one place. It sounds like accounting homework until you’re two bad months from zero runway and realize you’ve been paying yourself like it’s 2019.

**Finance got more honest.** Under Finance you’ll see more ways to stay alive or grow: bank debt that actually behaves like debt, revenue-based financing, SAFEs that convert when you raise, and the usual accelerator / priced-round mess. None of it is a spreadsheet simulator—the pacing is still “tycoon month”—but the tradeoffs are sharper than “click raise and receive money.”

**Pre-launch isn’t just a countdown.** Before you ship, you can run crowdfunding-style desk tasks that stack tiny recurring revenue, buzz, and product progress. It’s a way to grind the awkward pre-revenue phase without faking a full product launch on day one.

**You can hire a COO.** Garage stage still can’t support a full exec bench, but once you’re scaling, you can bring in a COO with a real name, salary, and an equity grant that hurts if you lowball them. Early on you can lean salary or equity—pick your poison.

**Desk work is bigger and less repetitive.** There are a lot more monthly tasks, split more clearly between product work and growth work. Side tasks unlock as you progress instead of hitting a wall at the same cap forever. You shouldn’t see the same card every third month; if you do, tell me—something’s wrong.

**New industry: space exploration.** Pick it at company setup if you want NASA contracts, launch milestones, and a rival that reads like SpaceXY fan fiction. It uses the same monthly loop; the flavor and task mix are what change.

**Accelerator demo day is in the loop.** Demo day is a real beat: pitch, terms, and consequences—not a cutscene. Batch codes on accelerators follow seasons (winter / spring / summer / fall) so intakes feel tied to the calendar.

**The calendar uses real months.** January is January. Sounds small until headlines and seasonality start lining up with how you think about a year.

**Numbers use commas.** Late-game revenue and valuations were technically correct and visually hostile. Trillions show as T; everything else groups digits like a normal human expects.

I’m still tuning balance—Sandbox exists if you want to stress-test without Career punching you in the face. Saves are local in your browser like before; no account.

If something reads wrong in the fake press, breaks after a refresh, or feels like a bug dressed as a feature, send a note. I’d rather fix it than pretend the sim is finished.
